The Dogwalk deploy runs on a self-hosted runner. That is great for speed and terrible for disk: every build leaves behind `node_modules`, `dist`, `coverage`, `test-reports`, plus a CI virtual environment. Add that up on every push and, within a few weeks, the machine that builds the project stops building anything. The debt came from an inventory taken on October 22 — the runner's work folder was the biggest offender on the list.

The fix looked trivial: a cleanup step in the workflow. It took me a little over seven hours and four commits to learn that it was not trivial at all, and that my first version was destroying the very job it existed to serve.

## What happened

On October 28, at 14:03, the disk guard lands (`63f17dcc`): before the build, measure free space and abort unless at least 5 GB is available, otherwise the build dies mid-run with an out-of-space error. In the same commit, a `Cleanup _work` step with `if: always()` ran `rm -rf` over the entire runner work folder, with a glob.

Eleven minutes later (`70affd9e`) I moved that cleanup to after the health check. It seemed reasonable: clean up once the pipeline had genuinely finished. Still red. At 20:11 (`4d1950eb`) the cleanup became the last step of the workflow, only after the notifications. Then the symptom became clear and indefensible: the notification step ran `git rev-parse` to build its message and died with `No such file or directory`. The checkout had been deleted out from under it.

The cause was this: on a self-hosted runner, the work folder is not a single folder. It holds the repository GitHub Actions just checked out **and** the `_actions` cache the steps load actions from. An `rm -rf` with a glob at the root wipes both. `Post Checkout code` — which runs after checkout — failed because the cache was gone, and every step that read the working directory after the cleanup inherited a directory that no longer existed.

## How it stands now

The fix has three pieces, and the most important one is not code.

The first is the scope of the cleanup. Instead of wiping the root, the step deletes only what is bulky and disposable, and nothing else:

```yaml
- name: Cleanup _work (frees disk; preserves repo and _actions)
  if: always()
  working-directory: /tmp
  run: |
    W="${GITHUB_WORKSPACE:-/home/runner/work/dog-walk/dog-walk}"
    for D in node_modules dist .venv-ci coverage test-reports; do
      rm -rf "$W/$D" 2>/dev/null || true
    done
    df -h / | tail -1
```

The detail that saves the step is `working-directory: /tmp`. A step has to be alive to run its command, and if its current directory has already been deleted, the shell never even starts. Pointing at a directory that genuinely exists before removing the rest is the difference between cleaning up and sabotaging.

The second piece is the disk guard, which learned the lesson: when space runs low it aborts the build but no longer deletes anything mid-path — only ephemeral runner junk. Cleaning in the middle of a job is precisely the behavior that broke the pipeline.

The third piece is a pre-commit hook (`da3e282b`). A script scans the workflows whenever one of them enters a commit and blocks the reintroduction of `rm -rf` with a glob on the work folder, with a message that says what is safe to clean. Two commits later, I tried to put the shortcut back in a branch cleanup and the hook stopped me. It was the best thing I wrote that day.

## Why it matters

A maintenance step is more dangerous than a feature step, because nobody reviews it. A feature breaks and everybody looks. Cleanup that erases the workspace seven steps ahead only shows up as red on something with nothing to do with disk — and the temptation is to blame the runner, the network, the npm cache. Those were seven hours walking backward from a symptom that lived far from the cause.

The rule that stuck: on a self-hosted runner, cleaning up is an operation over what you know, never over a glob. And no cleanup runs in the middle of a job — if it is meant to free disk, it is the last step, when there is nothing left to break.

## Next step

The runner still fills up. The next measure is pruning the `_actions` cache by age, with a separate scheduled job, instead of pushing that responsibility into the deploy. And the workflow hook will gain one more rule: block `rm -rf` without an explicit `working-directory`, which is how the defect comes back in disguise.
