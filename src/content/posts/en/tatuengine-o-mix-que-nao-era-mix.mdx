## The arm that read what nobody else read

The distillation dataset has four arms. Three build pairs out of everyday conversation. The C arm reads papers and code: long documents, vocabulary that never shows up in dialogue — the material that teaches a model to reason about technical matter instead of answering a loose question.

On October 18, an audit of the 18,739-pair dataset found the C arm at 0%. Not a thin slice: zero. The teacher was not failing, the queue was not stuck, the script ran to the end writing pairs for the other arms. The C arm did not exist because the corpus it reads lived on a path the machine migration had erased.

## The corpus was one path away

The loader had a list of candidate paths and stopped at the first one that existed, starting with `/mnt/d/...` from the old machine. After the migration that disk is gone — and since a missing corpus was treated as a normal case, it moved on without a corpus, fell back to the alternate arm mix, and the campaign kept looking "healthy" while producing pairs for A, B and D.

The fix is in commit `3a36396`: two new paths went ahead of the old one, pointing at the current data disk. The same commit added `D2_BRACOS`, which restricts a campaign to a single arm, and a guard that explicitly refuses the C arm without a corpus — with a stderr message, instead of silently dropping the arm from the mix.

With the corpus found, the numbers showed up: 141 documents, 111 of them code and 30 papers. The splitter discards anything under 800 characters — 140 survive — and cuts 600-character windows with a 1,400 stride. Result: 3,667 technical slices that have to become question, reasoning and answer pairs.

## 766 pairs, 19 thousand attempts

The campaign targeted 6,200 pairs with 16 threads. The manifest reads: 766 new pairs, 997 burned indices, 19,352 attempts that produced no pair, 12,841 seconds — 3h34. Together with what was already on disk from earlier runs, the file closed at 6,272 lines from distinct indices.

The number that matters is not 766, it is the ratio: 12,841 seconds for 766 pairs is 16.8 seconds per pair. And the 12,400 indices walked — twice the target, the queue's work ceiling — produced 6,272 pairs: half was discarded.

The engine tries four times per index and switches teacher on every attempt, advancing seven positions in the queue: `pick_teacher(i + attempt * 7)`. That rotation exists because the teacher used to be fixed per index, and a lane with no quota would burn that index forever — four attempts on the same model, zero conversion. After the fourth failure the index is abandoned and counted under `fail`.

## The declared mix and the mix that shipped

The teacher mix is written as `2:2:1` — two shares for the main lane of a free aggregator, two for a small Cohere model, one for a Laguna. If the proportion held, the file would be 40%, 40% and 20%.

The real count across the 6,272 pairs:

- 6,078 pairs (96.9%) from the aggregator's main lane;
- 164 pairs (2.6%) from Cohere;
- 30 pairs (0.5%) from Laguna.

That is not a mix: it is one lane with two guests that showed up in 3% of the lines. Rotation guarantees every index passes through different teachers, not that all of them deliver — whoever answers, answers; whoever returns a quota error, leaves fast. The weight declared in the code has no mechanism forcing it: it is intent, not control.

Diversity was being treated as a property of the file, when it is a consequence of how the lanes behaved that day: if the main lane goes down tomorrow, the file changes shape without a single configuration line being touched.

## The manifest measures success, not yield

The manifest counts `ok`, `fail`, `retry`. It does not count distribution per teacher, it does not count yield per index, it does not say which arm produced what. A manifest with 766 pairs and no error looks healthy, and it is — for what it measures. The 997 burned indices and the 19,352 attempts sit there as three loose numbers, with no rate and no owner.

The same blindness showed up in the quality filter. The validator requires `self_score` of at least 8, prompt from 25 to 350 characters, reasoning from 60 to 480, answer from 40 to 360. On September 16, 352 measured pairs showed teachers returning a score on the 0-to-100 scale instead of 0 to 10. Without normalization, a pair scored 85 would be read as 85 and the filter would become decoration, approving everything. The fix divides by ten above 10; the lesson stays: a metric crossing a system boundary needs a scale test, not trust.

## What stays

The C arm went from 0% to 6,272 pairs, the corpus sits on the right disk and the guard keeps it from vanishing again without notice. What is not resolved is the question the count raised: 96.9% of the pairs come from a single teacher. That may not matter — maybe quality comes from the corpus, not from whoever turns it into a pair — or it may be the difference between diversity and a monoculture with three names in the header.

The next step is not generating more pairs: it is measuring whether the distribution changes what the model learns — train two samples, one as-is and one balanced per teacher, and compare on the holdout. Without that, "teacher mix" stays a sentence in the code, not a property of the dataset.
