## Context

The TatuEngine SFT (Supervised Fine-Tuning) pipeline is the project's current bottleneck. The Teacher LoRA (Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct + LoRA R=16) has already distilled 381 reasoning examples into 3 datasets. The Student (BitMamba-1B PyTorch) has the model — 1.446B parameters, 48 layers — but **it doesn't learn properly**.

It's not a lack of data. The problem is more fundamental: **training systematically explodes into NaN**.

There were 4 distinct bugs. Each killed training in a different way. And Adafactor (the AdamW substitute) was simultaneously part of the solution and part of the problem.

---

## The 4 NaN Fixes

### Fix #1 — Softplus overflow in dt_bias

The first NaN came from the SSM step — layer 1, first batch, always at `ssm_step`. The culprit: `softplus(dt_bias + dt)`.

In 32-bit floating point, `exp()` blows up close to the type's upper limit. Once `dt_bias` crossed that band after a few iterations, softplus returned `+inf`, the `ssm_step` propagated `inf`, and backward turned everything into NaN.

2 days of debugging. The cure is classic: swap the naive softplus formulation for the numerically stable version, which rewrites the expression to stay away from overflow.

### Fix #2 — Adafactor and the `rsqrt(0)` in bf16

Adafactor factorizes the second moment into rank-1 (row + col), saving ~4 GB vs AdamW (4 GB vs 8.14 GB on RTX 3060). But there's a catch:

The optimizer's default epsilon was tuned for the float32 world, where the smallest representable number is tiny. In bf16 the picture changes: that same epsilon goes subnormal, collapses to zero inside the `rsqrt`, and you get `inf * 0 = NaN` in the early steps, when the second moment is still small.

The cure: instead of pinning a number, let the optimizer derive epsilon from the dtype itself — the library exposes a per-type floor that prevents underflow in `rsqrt`. The exact value matters less than the principle: **epsilon has to track the precision of the type**.

### Fix #3 — State LR Scale: state params need a much higher LR

The SSM state parameters (`A_log`, `D`, `dt_bias`) control the model's temporal dynamics. At the run's base learning rate they barely move — their scale lives in a different numerical world from the projection weights.

Without a dedicated multiplier, `dt_bias` freezes near 0, softplus sticks at a dead value forever, and the model never develops temporal discretization — the SSM essentially doesn't work.

### Fix #4 — Gradient Clamping: clamp + max-norm

The SSM gradient is inherently unstable because of backpropagation through the discretization `exp(Δ · A)`.

In head-only mode (training only the lm_head), gradients are left free — no clamp because the head needs fast movement to tame output variance. In full SFT the clamp is mandatory: without it, deep layers explode between step 5 and 10.

## Comparison Table: Adafactor vs AdamW

| Aspect | AdamW | Adafactor |
|--------|-------|-----------|
| Optimizer memory | 8.14 GB (2 states + momentum) | ~4 GB (rank-1 factorized) |
| Cost per step | Higher (full matrix update) | Lower (row/col update) |
| bf16 stability | default epsilon safe | Needs dtype-derived epsilon |
| Warmup | Required (cold momentum) | Built-in (`relative_step=True`) |
| Convergence | Faster in transformers | Slower in SSM (needs a per-state-group LR scale) |

The 4 GB saving is critical on the RTX 3060 (12 GB). With AdamW, the 1.4B model + optimizer take ~11.5 GB — no room for batch > 1 or sequences > 256 tokens. With Adafactor, 7.5 GB fit and there's headroom.

## Lessons learned

1. **Softplus isn't safe in float32** — the naive formulation explodes close to the type's upper bound. Always use the numerically stable version.

2. **Adafactor saves VRAM but charges in tuning** — epsilon must be calibrated to the dtype. Letting the library derive it works; the float32-tuned default breaks in bf16.

losing training runs along the way

4. **Gradient clamping isn't optional in SSM** — backprop through the discretization amplifies gradients exponentially. Per-parameter clamping + global norm clipping are complementary safety layers, not redundant.

The full Student SFT hasn't run yet (3 epochs, 381 examples). But the pipeline no longer explodes — the next step is letting it train and seeing what comes out.

> TL;DR: Adafactor saved 4 GB of VRAM (critical on the RTX 3060), but cost 3 lost training runs to NaN until the 4 fixes were right. Numerically stable softplus, dtype-derived epsilon, a dedicated LR for SSM state parameters, and clamp+clip as complementary safety layers. The pipeline no longer explodes — now let it run.
